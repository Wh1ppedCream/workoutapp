Tonos Expressive menus and pop-ups

A map of the overlay patterns currently present in the app, with examples of how their behavior and presentation differ.

Your impression is accurate. Expressive currently has several separate menu and overlay implementations. They serve different jobs, but their surfaces, outlines, shapes, spacing, and action treatments are not all driven by one shared overlay system. Menus

1. Action pop-ups — PopupMenuButton

Anchored to an overflow or row action control. It presents commands such as Activate, Rename, Delete, Edit, or Archive. These are action lists; they do not represent a selected field value.

  • Archived plan card: recent custom lavender/plum menu, leading icon tiles, semantic red Delete, and a separated destructive action.
  • Plan detail and health entries: separate popup-menu styling and item hierarchy. Historical captures show a warmer/default-looking surface and simpler rows.
  • Enabled/disabled states depend on the action and current record state.

2. Custom anchored selectors — MenuAnchor

Anchored to a choice field, usually sized to that field and styled locally. This gives the screen control over width, maximum height, scrolling, outside taps, and focus behavior.

  • Used for Volume Boundaries, Flow Methods, Body Part / Muscle Mapping, Exercise Editor equipment, and plan-flow choices.
  • The implementation is similar, but the menus choose different token surfaces: configuration, tertiary, or accent.
  • Selection marks and item density also vary by caller.

3. Field dropdowns — DropdownButton

These open a selection route from inside forms and dialogs. They inherit more of the Material dropdown sizing and menu behavior than the custom MenuAnchor selectors.

  • Exercise Catalog Filters uses them for workspace profile, equipment, body part, and muscle.
  • Dialog form fields also use TonosDialogDropdownButton, which applies a theme-aware surface and rounded Expressive menu corners.
  • The open menu can occupy a much larger viewport area than its trigger field, especially for long lists.

Dialogs

4. Destination-themed alert dialogs

Ordinary AlertDialog content inherits a destination-scoped Expressive dialog theme: tertiary surface and foreground tokens, an asymmetric rounded shape, and an accent outline. Each screen still supplies its own title, body, and actions.

TonosDialogFrame does not provide this Expressive treatment; it returns its child unchanged for Expressive and supplies additional theming for Neo.

5. Task-specific confirmations and form dialogs

Several flows compose their own layout inside a dialog. The archived-plan confirmation is now a distinct custom layout: named question, warning icon, explanatory text, neutral Cancel, and semantic red Delete. Form dialogs add inputs, validation, date/time controls, or long scrolling content.

  • The archived-plan delete dialog uses the planning surface and custom layout.
  • Plan Detail has a separate delete confirmation implementation, even though it performs the same kind of action.
  • Measurement entry dialogs use form-focused layouts, with nested date/time pickers.

6. Choice dialogs and native pickers

TonosChoiceDialog wraps an alert dialog around a list of radio-style choices, sometimes with a preview or subtitle. Date and time selectors use Flutter's calendar and clock picker routes. These are modal selection tasks, not anchored dropdowns.

Both share the dialog route, but the choice list and native picker controls have their own row geometry, selection marks, and content density.

Sheets and full-screen viewers

7. Modal bottom sheets

TonosSheet is a reusable token-driven shell with a top handle, optional title/close action, safe area, and theme surface. In product flows, however, the main sheets are custom widgets that control their own header, height, scrolling, drag behavior, and surfaces.

  • Exercise Details is a tall, draggable multi-tab sheet.
  • Swap Exercise, Automatic Settings, and Session Complete use their own feature-specific sheet content.
  • Current source search found TonosSheet used by Theme Lab examples; production feature sheets call modal bottom-sheet routes directly.

Why they look different

  • There is no single Expressive menu theme. Source defines popup styling in individual PopupMenuButton or MenuAnchor call sites; no global Expressive PopupMenuThemeData or dropdown menu theme was found.
  • Three separate menu mechanics are in use. Action pop-ups, anchored custom selectors, and field dropdown routes have different positioning and sizing rules.
  • Dialog colors are shared more than dialog layouts. A destination wrapper sets the Expressive AlertDialog theme, but each caller still chooses its own layout; custom dialog surfaces can also override it.
  • The reusable sheet shell has limited product coverage. Most user-facing sheets use feature-specific widgets and shapes.
  • Same action, different confirmation: archived plan deletion and Plan Detail deletion currently use separate confirmation layouts.

A unified system can keep the right interaction for each job—anchored choices remain menus, confirmations remain dialogs, and large content remains sheets—while sharing tokenized surfaces, outlines, shapes, row heights, selected/disabled states, and destructive action hierarchy.
